Section A: Short Type Questions

1\. a) Explain the scenario of a retail store in India.

The retail store scenario in India is characterized by a "phygital" model, blending organized modern trade (supermarkets, malls, brand outlets) with unorganized traditional trade (local kirana stores). Modern retail relies heavily on visual merchandising, automated Point of Sale (POS) systems, omni-channel integration, and data analytics to optimize customer footfall and inventory turnover.

1\. b) Why is a store operations floor walk an integral part of store operations?

A floor walk is a systematic routine where managers walk through the sales floor to audit store readiness. It is critical because it ensures:

&#x20;\* Visual standards and display aesthetics (planograms) are maintained.

&#x20;\* Product replenishment occurs, preventing stockouts on key displays.

&#x20;\* Safety, cleanliness, and security protocols are adhered to.

&#x20;\* Staff members are actively engaging customers and following service protocols.

2\. a) Define the terms customer and consumer and explain them with an example.

&#x20;\* Customer: The individual or entity that purchases the product or service.

&#x20;\* Consumer: The ultimate end-user who actually consumes or uses the product.

&#x20;\* Example: A parent buying baby food from a retail outlet is the customer, while the baby who eats the food is the consumer.

2\. b) Describe different types of retail customers.

&#x20;\* Loyal Customers: Highly engaged shoppers who consistently prefer your store over competitors.

&#x20;\* Discount/Bargain Hunters: Shoppers driven purely by deals, clearance sales, and discounts.

&#x20;\* Impulse Buyers: Customers purchasing items without prior planning, influenced by visual displays or point-of-purchase placement.

&#x20;\* Need-Based Customers: Shoppers with a specific purchase intent who buy only what is on their list.

&#x20;\* Wandering/Browser Customers: Shoppers visiting for leisure without an immediate intention to purchase.

3\. a) Discuss the importance of human resource management in Retail.

HRM drives store success because retail relies directly on frontline personnel to shape customer experience. Key aspects include:

&#x20;\* Controlling high employee turnover through engagement and clear career paths.

&#x20;\* Ensuring staff are trained in product knowledge, cashiering, and customer service skills.

&#x20;\* Optimizing staff scheduling based on peak and non-peak footfall hours to control labor costs.

3\. b) What are the different sources of potential candidates?

&#x20;\* Internal Sources: Employee promotions, internal job postings, lateral transfers, and employee referrals.

&#x20;\* External Sources: Campus recruitment drives, online job portals, recruitment agencies, social media networks (LinkedIn), walk-in interviews, and local job advertisements.

4\. a) What are the advantages and disadvantages of having a centralized warehouse?

| Advantages | Disadvantages |

|---|---|

| Bulk storage lowers total operational \& holding costs | Higher shipping costs and delivery times to remote stores |

| Consistent inventory control \& easier quality checks | Single point of failure (disruptions stop supply across all locations) |

| Optimized labor \& automated warehousing equipment | Reduced responsiveness to localized emergency demands |

4\. b) Explain the process of calculating GMROI.

Gross Margin Return on Investment (GMROI) measures an inventory's ability to convert raw inventory investment into gross margin profits.

&#x20;\* Step 1: Calculate Gross Margin = \\text{Total Sales} - \\text{Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)}.

&#x20;\* Step 2: Determine Average Inventory Cost over a given period = \\frac{\\text{Beginning Inventory Cost} + \\text{Ending Inventory Cost}}{2}.

&#x20;\* Step 3: Divide Gross Margin by Average Inventory Cost to measure profit yield per inventory dollar.

5\. a) Define space planning concept. What process must be followed for well-defined space planning?

Space planning involves strategically allocating retail floor area to product categories to maximize sales per square foot and optimize shopper traffic.

&#x20;\* Process for Space Planning:

&#x20;  \* Analyze sales performance and category contribution data.

&#x20;  \* Map out overall store layout (grid, free-form, or loop floor plans).

&#x20;  \* Determine category space allocations based on sales volume and margin contribution.

&#x20;  \* Design planograms to define specific shelf placements for individual SKUs.

&#x20;  \* Review footfall patterns and continuously adapt product placement.

5\. b) Explain the concept of "Space Mix".

Space Mix refers to the strategic balance between selling space (merchandise displays, aisles, checkout queues) and non-selling space (stockrooms, fitting rooms, employee break areas, administrative offices). Achieving the right space mix ensures operational efficiency without sacrificing customer shopping comfort or sales potential.

6\. a) What are the three categories of assets? Explain.

&#x20;\* Current Assets: Short-term liquid assets converted to cash within one year (e.g., inventory, cash reserves, accounts receivable).

&#x20;\* Non-Current / Fixed Assets: Long-term tangible assets critical for operational functions (e.g., store properties, display fixtures, POS hardware).

&#x20;\* Intangible Assets: Non-physical assets carrying commercial value (e.g., brand trademark, store goodwill, proprietary retail software).

6\. b) Explain the different methods of calculating depreciation values.

&#x20;\* Straight-Line Method: Distributes equal depreciation cost across every year of an asset's useful life.

&#x20;  

&#x20;\* Written-Down Value (Declining Balance) Method: Applies a fixed percentage of depreciation to the remaining book value each year, leading to higher initial expense charges.

&#x20;\* Units of Production Method: Calculates depreciation based on actual operational usage (e.g., running hours of equipment or vehicle mileage).

7\. a) Explain the purpose and benefits of Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs).

SOPs are step-by-step written instructions designed to maintain consistency in store execution.

&#x20;\* Benefits:

&#x20;  \* Ensures operational consistency and service quality across multiple store locations.

&#x20;  \* Reduces training times and onboarding friction for new hires.

&#x20;  \* Minimizes human errors, shrinkage, and safety hazards.

7\. b) Explain the Transactions Drivers and their significance in retail.

Transaction drivers are key performance metrics that directly govern overall revenue performance.

&#x20;\* Key Drivers: Total Store Footfall, Conversion Rate (percentage of visitors making a purchase), Average Basket Value (ABV), and Units Per Transaction (UPT).

&#x20;\* Significance: Identifying these drivers helps managers target specific bottlenecks (e.g., if footfall is high but conversion is low, staff engagement or pricing strategy needs adjustment).

Section B: Essay Type Questions

8\. What do you understand by the term cashiering? Explain the various qualities of a good cashier with the help of an example.

Cashiering is the process of handling financial transactions at the POS counter, encompassing payment processing (cash, card, UPI), scanning items, issuing receipts, applying discounts, and handling daily cash reconciliations.

&#x20;\* Qualities of a Good Cashier:

&#x20;  \* Accuracy and Attention to Detail: Prevents billing errors and cash register discrepancies.

&#x20;  \* Speed \& Efficiency: Keeps checkout queues moving during peak store traffic.

&#x20;  \* Customer Orientation \& Interpersonal Skills: Acts as the final touchpoint for customer experience, leaving a positive last impression.

&#x20;  \* Honesty and Integrity: Safeguards customer payment details and internal register cash.

&#x20;\* Example: During a weekend rush at a clothing store, a customer presents a promotional discount voucher along with a basket of items. An efficient cashier swiftly scans items, accurately applies the promotion without holding up the line, confirms total savings to the customer with a pleasant demeanor, and hands over a neatly bagged purchase—turning a potentially slow queue into a positive final impression.

9\. Write the benefits of the offline and online integrations? How Ecommerce or online business changes the buying habits of the consumers? Explain with example.

Benefits of Offline \& Online Integration (Omnichannel Retail):

&#x20;\* Unified Inventory Visibility: Stores function as local fulfillment centers for online orders (BOPIS: Buy Online, Pick Up In-Store).

&#x20;\* Enhanced Customer Convenience: Allows consumers to browse products virtually and test them in-person, or initiate returns at physical locations.

&#x20;\* Cross-Channel Data Insights: Combines digital browsing behavior with physical purchasing patterns to personalize promotional campaigns.

Shift in Consumer Buying Habits:

Ecommerce has shifted consumer expectations toward 24/7 availability, instant product price comparisons, personalized product recommendations, and frictionless home delivery.

&#x20;\* Example: A shopper looking for a leather jacket browses designs on a brand’s mobile app late at night, compares prices and customer reviews, orders their specific size for click-and-collect, and stops by the physical store the next day to try it on and complete the purchase. This demonstrates how integrated channels turn routine shopping into an instant, highly flexible experience.
